Every domain has public ownership information called **WHOIS**. Domain privacy hides
your personal details from that public record. Here's what's exposed, what privacy
does, and when you can and can't use it.

## What WHOIS is

When you register a domain, the registry stores contact details for the
**registrant** (owner), plus admin and technical contacts. Historically, anyone
could look these up — name, address, email, phone. That's the WHOIS record.

Without protection, that means your personal details can be visible to spammers,
scammers, and anyone who's curious.

## What domain privacy does

**Domain privacy** (also called WHOIS privacy or ID protection) replaces your
personal details in the public record with a privacy service's details, while the
registry still knows the real owner is you. You keep full control of the domain;
the public just can't see who you are.

Turn it on from **Domains → Manage → Privacy**. On many endings it's free or
included; we'll show you at checkout and in the domain settings.

## When privacy isn't available

Privacy depends on the domain ending and registry rules:

- Some country endings (for example certain `.uk` registrations) **don't allow**
  full privacy, or handle it differently.
- Registries may require valid, verifiable registrant details regardless.

If privacy isn't offered for your domain, that's a registry rule, not a Scale
Hosting choice.

## Modern WHOIS and GDPR

Since GDPR, most personal data in WHOIS for individuals is **redacted by default** —
registries and registrars limit what's shown publicly. Privacy protection adds an
extra layer, but even without it, less is exposed than in the past. Businesses that
register domains in an organisation's name may still appear.

## Turn privacy off temporarily for transfers

When you **transfer a domain** in or out, the confirmation email sometimes goes to
the registrant address in WHOIS. If privacy is masking that, briefly turn privacy
**off**, complete the transfer, then turn it back **on**. See *Transferring a domain
to us* and *Transferring a domain away*.

## Looking up a domain

You can run a WHOIS lookup on any domain to see its registrar, creation/expiry
dates, and nameservers — useful when you're chasing down where a domain is managed
or when it expires.

## Good practice

- **Keep privacy on** for personal domains — there's rarely a reason not to.
- **Keep your real details accurate** behind the privacy screen; false registrant
  info can get a domain suspended (see *Updating your profile and contact details*).
- **Verify registrant emails** when asked — ICANN requires it, privacy or not.
