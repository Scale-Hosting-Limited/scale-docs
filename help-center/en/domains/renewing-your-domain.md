A domain is yours only for the period you've paid for. Let it lapse and your
website and email stop working — and eventually someone else can register it. This
guide keeps that from happening.

## Auto-renew (the easy way)

The safest option is to let it renew itself.

1. Go to **Domains → Manage** and select the domain.
2. Turn on **auto-renew**.
3. Make sure you have a **valid payment method** on file (see *Updating your
   payment details*).

We'll attempt the renewal automatically before expiry and email you the invoice.
Nothing to remember, nothing to lose.

## Renewing manually

Prefer to pay each time?

1. Go to **Domains → Manage** (or **Billing → Invoices** if there's a renewal
   invoice waiting).
2. Select the domain and choose **Renew**, picking how many years to add.
3. Pay the invoice. The new expiry date updates once payment clears.

We send renewal reminders by email as expiry approaches — keep your account email
current so you actually get them (see *Changing your account email*).

## What happens if it expires

Domains don't vanish the second they expire, but the clock starts:

1. **Expiry** — the domain stops working (site and email go down). It can usually
   still be renewed normally for a short grace period.
2. **Redemption period** — after the grace period, renewing costs a **redemption
   fee** set by the registry, and it's a manual recovery.
3. **Release** — eventually the domain is deleted and anyone can register it. At
   this point it may be gone for good.

The lesson: renew early. Don't rely on the redemption period — it's expensive and
not guaranteed.

## Renew for multiple years

You can add several years at once. This is worth doing for a domain you rely on —
it reduces the chance of a lapse and locks in the registration.

## Troubleshooting

- **Renewal payment failed:** update your payment method and retry the invoice; see
  *Failed payments and overdue services*.
- **Didn't get a reminder:** check spam, confirm your account email, and turn on
  auto-renew as a backstop.
- **Domain shows expired but you paid:** allow a little time for the registry to
  update; if it persists, open a ticket with the domain name.
