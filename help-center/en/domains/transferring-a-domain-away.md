It's your domain, and you can move it to another registrar whenever you like. We
don't hold domains hostage — here's how to transfer one out cleanly.

## What you'll need

To transfer a domain away, the **gaining** (new) registrar will ask you for:

1. The **domain name**.
2. The **authorisation code** (EPP / auth code) — you get this from us.
3. The domain to be **unlocked** at our end.

## Get your auth code and unlock

1. Go to **Domains → Manage** and select the domain.
2. **Unlock** the domain (turn off registrar lock).
3. Request/copy the **authorisation code**. If privacy is on and it blocks the
   confirmation email, turn privacy off for the duration.
4. Make sure the **admin email** on the domain is one you can access — approvals
   often go there.

Then start the transfer at your new registrar and give them the auth code.

## Timing rules

Registry rules mean a domain **can't be transferred** if:

- It was registered or last transferred **within the last 60 days**.
- It's **expired** and in a redemption period — renew it first.

Most outbound transfers complete within a few days. You can usually **approve** the
transfer to speed it along rather than waiting for the automatic release.

## Don't lose your DNS or email

Transferring away moves management to the new registrar — it may **not** carry over
your DNS records. Before you go:

- **Export/note your DNS records** (A, CNAME, MX, TXT) from *Managing DNS records*.
- Recreate them at the new registrar (or wherever your DNS will live) so your site
  and email keep working.

## If you're leaving because of a problem

If something's not working and that's why you're moving, we'd genuinely like the
chance to fix it — open a ticket first. But if you've decided, we'll help the
transfer go smoothly. We won't refuse a valid transfer request.

## After it's gone

Once the domain leaves us it's managed entirely by the new registrar — renewals,
DNS, and privacy all happen there. If you have hosting or a server still with us,
point the domain back at it from the new registrar (see *Pointing your domain*).
