This is a friendly summary to help you stay on the right side of the rules. The
**Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policy are the authoritative documents** — if
anything here and there differ, they win. If you're ever unsure whether something
is allowed, ask us first.

## The short version

Run your games, websites, bots, and apps. Don't use our servers to harm other
people, other networks, or the shared infrastructure everyone relies on.

## Not allowed

**Attacks and abuse of others**
- Denial-of-service (DoS/DDoS) traffic, port scanning, or attacks on other systems.
- Spam or bulk unsolicited email/messaging.
- Credential stuffing, brute-forcing, or hacking tools aimed at third parties.

**Abusing the shared platform**
- **Cryptocurrency mining** and similar "use 100% CPU forever for profit"
  workloads — they steal performance from everyone on the node.
- Deliberately evading resource limits, or running background compute farms.
- Tunnels/proxies/VPNs used to disguise or relay abusive traffic.

**Illegal or harmful content**
- Anything illegal where you or your users are, including pirated software/content
  and material that exploits or endangers others.
- Malware distribution.

**Nested/disguised systems**
- Running a full nested OS or virtualisation to sidestep the service you bought.

## Grey areas — just ask

Some things are fine in moderation but can look like abuse at scale (heavy
automation, large outbound connection counts, unusual traffic). If you're planning
something that might trip an alarm, open a ticket first and we'll tell you how to
run it cleanly.

## How we enforce it

We monitor for the active-harm cases above (mining, attacks, spam relays) because
they hurt other customers and can get the whole node's IP blacklisted. Our
automated system's strongest action is to **suspend** a service (never delete it),
and **a human reviews every suspension**. You can always appeal — see
*Understanding suspensions and appeals*.

Most issues are honest mistakes (a misconfigured plugin, a runaway process). We'd
much rather help you fix it than penalise you, so if you get a warning or a
suspension, read the reason and reply — that's what the appeal is for.
