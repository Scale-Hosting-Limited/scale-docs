If one of your services is suspended, it's paused — not deleted. This guide
explains why it happens and how to get it back.

## Suspension vs termination

- A **suspension** stops the service temporarily. Your files, world, and data are
  intact and untouched. It's reversible.
- Our automated abuse system's **strongest** action is suspend — it can never
  delete or terminate your data. That's a deliberate, hard limit.

## Why a service gets suspended

**Billing.** The most common reason: an invoice went unpaid. Pay the outstanding
invoice and the service is reinstated (usually automatically). See *Managing your
subscription*.

**Abuse detection.** If our monitoring sees active-harm behaviour — cryptomining,
attack traffic, a spam relay — it can suspend the service to protect the node and
other customers. **A human reviews every automated suspension**, and you're told
the reason.

**Manual action.** Occasionally staff suspend a service directly for a clear terms
violation or a security concern (for example, a compromised account sending spam).

## What happens to my data?

Nothing — it stays exactly as it was. A suspended game server can't be joined and a
suspended site won't serve, but the files are safe and come straight back when the
suspension is lifted. (If you'd asked us to delete something, that's a separate,
explicit process.)

## How to appeal

Every enforcement suspension comes with a **reference** and a way to respond:

1. Read the reason you were given — it names what was detected.
2. Open the **appeal** linked in the notice (or open a support ticket quoting the
   reference).
3. Explain what happened. If it was a mistake on our side or a false positive, say
   so; if it was a misconfigured plugin or a runaway process, tell us what you've
   changed so it won't recur.

A person reviews the appeal. If it was a false positive or you've fixed the cause,
we reinstate the service — often with no strike. Our detection even learns from
these outcomes, so a detector that keeps getting appeals overturned is
automatically trusted less over time.

## Avoiding suspensions

- Keep invoices paid (enable a payment method and watch renewal notices).
- Stay within the acceptable-use rules — see *Acceptable use*.
- If you plan something that might look like abuse (heavy automation, big outbound
  traffic), ask us first.
- Secure your account with 2FA so it can't be misused by someone else.

If you think a suspension is wrong, appeal — don't just re-open duplicate tickets.
The appeal goes to a human and is the fastest route to getting back online.
