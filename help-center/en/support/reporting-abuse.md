If a service hosted with us is being used to harm others — attacks, illegal content,
spam, or fraud — we want to know. This explains what to report, how, and what we do
with it.

## What counts as abuse

Report activity that breaks our Acceptable Use Policy or the law, such as:

- **Attacks** — DDoS, hacking attempts, port scanning, or brute-forcing originating
  from a service we host.
- **Illegal content** — including anything that exploits or endangers minors, which
  we act on immediately and report to the authorities.
- **Spam and phishing** — mass unsolicited email, or pages built to steal
  credentials.
- **Malware distribution** — hosting or spreading malicious software.
- **Fraud and scams** — fake shops, payment scams, impersonation.
- **Harassment** targeting individuals.

For the full rules, see *Acceptable use and prohibited content*.

## How to report

Send an abuse report through a **support ticket** (see *Opening a support ticket*),
or the dedicated abuse contact if one is listed. Include as much as you can:

- **What** is happening (the type of abuse).
- **Where** — the IP address, domain, or URL involved. This is the most important
  detail; without it we may not be able to identify the service.
- **When** — dates and times, with timezone, especially for attacks or logs.
- **Evidence** — log excerpts, email headers, screenshots. Don't include anything
  illegal itself; describe and reference it instead.

The more precise the detail, the faster we can act.

## What we do with a report

1. **Review** the report and identify the service involved.
2. **Investigate** — we look at the evidence and our own logs.
3. **Act** — depending on severity, that can mean contacting the customer to fix it,
   suspending the service, or, for serious/illegal activity, immediate suspension
   and reporting to the relevant authorities.

Serious abuse — especially anything involving child safety or active attacks — is
acted on with urgency.

## Privacy and confidentiality

We take reports seriously and handle them discreetly. We don't share a reporter's
personal details with the reported customer beyond what's legally required. We may
not be able to share the outcome due to privacy obligations, but rest assured
credible reports are investigated.

## If your own service is flagged

If we contact you about suspected abuse on your service, respond promptly — it may
be a compromised server rather than intentional misuse. See *Understanding
suspensions and appeals* for how that process works and how to put things right.

## Emergencies

If someone is in **immediate danger**, contact your local emergency services first.
Then report the online element to us and, where relevant, the appropriate national
reporting body.
