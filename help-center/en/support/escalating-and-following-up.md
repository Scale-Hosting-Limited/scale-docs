Most tickets are handled in one smooth conversation. Occasionally something stalls,
or an issue needs more attention. Here's how to follow up effectively and escalate
when it's genuinely warranted — without slowing your own ticket down.

## Give it a reasonable window first

Before chasing, allow time in line with our stated response targets (see *Our
response times and priorities*). During incidents or busy periods, replies can take
longer while we focus on restoring service for everyone. A little patience often
means your answer is already on the way.

## Following up the right way

- **Reply on the same ticket.** Keep everything in one thread so the whole history
  stays together.
- **Don't open duplicate tickets** for the same issue — in many systems that splits
  the conversation and pushes things back, slowing you down.
- **Add new information**, not just "any update?" — anything that's changed, more
  detail, or answers to our earlier questions moves it forward.
- **Reply promptly** when we ask for something; a ticket waiting on you can't
  progress.

## When to escalate

It's reasonable to ask for escalation when:

- The ticket has gone **well beyond** our normal response window with no reply.
- You believe the issue is **more serious or urgent** than it's being treated.
- A previous resolution **didn't actually fix** the problem.

## How to escalate

1. **Reply on the existing ticket** and clearly ask for it to be escalated,
   explaining why (the impact, the delay, or what's still unresolved).
2. **Summarise** what's happened so far so a new person can pick it up quickly.
3. **Be specific about impact** — "my production site has been down for X hours" tells
   us how to prioritise.

We'll get the right person on it and keep you updated.

## Keep it constructive

We understand a stuck issue is frustrating, and we'd rather fix it than defend it.
Escalations go faster when they're clear and factual — the impact, the timeline, and
what you need. Aggression or duplicate tickets don't speed things up; clarity does.

## If you're still not satisfied

If you've escalated and remain unhappy with the outcome, say so on the ticket and
ask for it to be reviewed by a team lead. For billing disputes, see *Cancellations
and refunds*; for data or privacy concerns, see *Your privacy and data rights*,
which also explains your right to raise a complaint with a supervisory authority.

## After resolution

Once it's sorted, a quick confirmation on the ticket helps us close it cleanly. If
the same problem returns, reopen the ticket (or reference it in a new one) so we have
the full history.
