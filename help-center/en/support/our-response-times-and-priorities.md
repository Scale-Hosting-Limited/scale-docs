When you open a ticket, it helps to know what to expect. This explains our target
response times and how we decide what to work on first, so you're never left
guessing.

## How we prioritise

Not every ticket is equally urgent, and treating them as if they were would slow
down the people who need help most. We generally prioritise like this:

1. **Platform outages** affecting many customers — highest priority, worked
   immediately.
2. **Service-down** issues affecting your service specifically.
3. **Account and billing** matters — verification, payments, suspensions.
4. **General questions and requests** — how-to, feature questions, non-urgent
   changes.

An outage that affects everyone will always come before a general question — that's
deliberate and fair.

## Response vs resolution

- **Response time** is how long until a human replies to your ticket.
- **Resolution time** is how long until it's fully fixed — which depends on the
  problem. A quick reply doesn't always mean an instant fix, but it does mean we're
  on it.

We aim to acknowledge tickets within our stated response targets and keep you
updated as we work.

## Get a faster answer

You can speed things up by giving us what we need up front:

- **Be specific** — the exact service, error message, and what you've tried (see
  *Opening a support ticket*).
- **One issue per ticket** — separate unrelated problems so each can be handled
  cleanly.
- **Reply promptly** when we ask for info — a ticket waiting on your answer can't
  progress.
- **Don't open duplicates** — multiple tickets for the same issue actually slow
  things down and split the conversation.

## Out-of-hours and busy periods

Response times can be longer outside normal hours and during incidents, when we're
focused on restoring service for everyone. During a known incident, check our status
notices before opening a ticket (see *Scheduled maintenance and status*).

## If it's urgent

Mark genuinely urgent issues clearly and explain the impact (e.g. "production
website down"). Reserve "urgent" for real urgency — if everything's marked urgent,
nothing is, and it slows the queue for everyone.

## Following up

If a ticket seems stuck, it's fine to reply for an update — but give us a reasonable
window first, and avoid bumping repeatedly, which pushes the ticket back in some
systems. To escalate properly, see *Escalating and following up*.
