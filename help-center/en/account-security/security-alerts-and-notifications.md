We send security notifications so you always know what's happening on your account.
Here's what they mean and what to do.

## Alerts you might get

- **New sign-in** — someone signed in, sometimes with device/location detail. If it
  was you, no action needed. If not, act immediately (below).
- **Password changed** — confirms a password change. If you didn't do it, treat it
  as a compromise.
- **Email changed** — confirms your account email was updated. If that wasn't you,
  contact us urgently.
- **2FA enabled/disabled** — confirms a change to two-factor. An unexpected
  *disable* is a red flag.
- **New payment method / billing change** — confirms billing updates.

## If an alert wasn't you

1. **Change your password** right away (or use "Forgot password" if you're already
   locked out).
2. **Sign out all sessions** (see *Managing active sessions and devices*).
3. **Re-enable 2FA / a passkey** and check your account email is still yours.
4. **Open a ticket** so we can review recent activity and help lock it down.

Speed matters — the sooner you act, the less an attacker can do.

## Managing which notifications you get

Under **Account → Notifications** you can control non-security messages (product
news, service notices). **Security-critical alerts are always sent** and can't be
turned off — that's deliberate, so you never miss a sign-in or a change to your
credentials.

## Make sure they reach you

- Keep your **account email** current and monitored (see *Changing your account
  email*).
- Add our address to your contacts so alerts don't get filtered to spam.
- If you stop receiving expected emails, check spam and your filters first.

## Real vs fake

Genuine alerts tell you what happened and let you act **by signing in normally** —
they don't demand you click a link and "re-enter your password" on some page. If a
"security alert" pushes you to log in through a link, treat it as phishing (see
*Recognising phishing and scams*) and go to the panel directly instead.
