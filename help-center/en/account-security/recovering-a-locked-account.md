title: Recovering a locked account
slug: recovering-a-locked-account
description: Get back in if you've lost your password, 2FA device, or email access.
---

Locked out? Work through these in order — most lockouts are recoverable on your
own.

## Forgot your password

Use **"Forgot password"** on the sign-in page. We email a reset link to your
account address. Follow it, set a new password, and sign in. Check spam if it
doesn't arrive within a few minutes.

## Lost your 2FA device

If you can't produce your authenticator code:

1. Use a **recovery code** — you were given a set when you enabled 2FA. Enter one in
   place of the code to sign in.
2. Once in, go to **Account → Security** and **reset 2FA** (set it up on your new
   device) and generate fresh recovery codes.

Keep recovery codes somewhere safe and offline — a password manager or a printout.
Each code works once.

## Lost your 2FA *and* your recovery codes

This is the hard case, by design — it's exactly the barrier that stops someone else
taking your account. Open a support ticket from the **email address on the
account**. We'll verify you're the owner before removing 2FA. Expect to answer some
ownership questions; the stricter we are here, the safer your account is.

## Lost access to your account email

If you can't receive password resets because the inbox is gone, open a ticket from
any address explaining the situation. We verify ownership (recent invoices, payment
details, services on the account) before changing the email. See *Changing your
account email*.

## Account seems compromised

If you can still get in but see changes you didn't make:

1. **Change your password** immediately.
2. **Sign out all sessions** (see *Managing active sessions and devices*).
3. **Check your account email** hasn't been changed, and re-enable 2FA/passkey.
4. Open a ticket so we can review recent activity.

## Prevent future lockouts

- Turn on **2FA or a passkey**, and **save your recovery codes** offline.
- Add **more than one passkey** (phone + laptop).
- Keep your **account email** current and secured with its own 2FA.

We will never ask for your password, and we'll only make recovery changes after
verifying you own the account — that protects you as much as it might slow you down.
