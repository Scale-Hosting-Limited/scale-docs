Your account controls all your services and billing, so a few minutes securing it
is the best time you'll spend. Here's the whole checklist.

## The essentials

1. **Turn on 2FA or a passkey.** This is the single biggest thing — it means a
   stolen password isn't enough to get in. See *Enabling two-factor
   authentication* and *Passkeys and social login*.
2. **Use a strong, unique password.** Long, random, and used nowhere else. A
   password manager makes this effortless. See *Changing your password*.
3. **Save your recovery codes** somewhere offline, so losing your phone doesn't
   lock you out.
4. **Secure your account email.** It can reset your password, so give it its own
   strong password and 2FA.

## Ongoing habits

- **Review active sessions** now and then, and sign out anything unfamiliar (see
  *Managing active sessions and devices*).
- **Be wary of phishing** — we never ask for your password or codes (see
  *Recognising phishing and scams*).
- **Don't share your login.** If someone needs limited access to a service, use
  sub-user access where available rather than handing over your account.
- **Keep contact details current** so security alerts and invoices reach you.

## For teams

- Give collaborators their **own** access (sub-users / their own accounts) instead
  of sharing yours — you can see who did what and revoke one person without
  changing everyone's login.
- Remove access promptly when someone leaves.

## If something's wrong

If you get an alert you don't recognise, see an unfamiliar session, or think your
account was accessed:

1. Change your password.
2. Sign out all sessions.
3. Confirm 2FA and your account email are intact.
4. Open a ticket.

See *Recovering a locked account* if you're locked out.

## The one-line version

**2FA on, a unique password, recovery codes saved, and a secured email address** —
do those four and you've handled almost all of the risk.
