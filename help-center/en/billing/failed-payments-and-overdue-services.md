Payments fail for ordinary reasons — an expired card, a mismatched address, a
temporary bank block. This explains what happens when one does, how long you have to
fix it, and how to get a suspended service back.

## Why payments fail

The usual culprits:

- **Expired card** — the most common; update it (see *Updating your payment
  details*).
- **Billing address mismatch** — the address on file doesn't match your bank's.
- **Insufficient funds or a limit** reached.
- **Bank blocked it** as unusual — a quick call to your bank clears this.
- **No default method** set for auto-renew.

## What happens after a failed payment

We don't cut you off the moment a payment fails. Typically:

1. **The invoice becomes overdue** and we notify you by email.
2. **We may retry** the payment automatically over the following days.
3. **Reminders** are sent so you can pay manually.
4. **After a grace period**, an unpaid service is **suspended** — paused but not yet
   deleted.
5. **After a longer period** of non-payment, a suspended service may be
   **terminated** and its data removed.

The exact timings are in our terms, but the message is simple: **pay the overdue
invoice promptly** and nothing is lost.

## Fix a failed payment

1. Go to **Billing → Invoices** and open the overdue one.
2. **Update or add** a valid payment method (see *Updating your payment details*).
3. **Pay** the invoice (see *Paying and viewing invoices*).
4. The service reactivates once payment clears.

## Restoring a suspended service

A **suspended** service is paused, not deleted. To restore it:

1. **Pay the outstanding invoice.**
2. The service is **reactivated**, usually automatically and quickly.
3. If it doesn't come back within a reasonable time after paying, open a ticket with
   the invoice number.

Your data is intact during suspension — but don't leave it, since prolonged
non-payment leads to termination.

## Avoiding it altogether

- **Keep a valid default method** on file and update cards before they expire.
- **Turn on auto-renew** so renewals happen without you (see *Managing your
  subscription*).
- **Keep your account email current** so you actually get the reminders (see
  *Changing your account email*).
- **Pay renewal invoices early** if you don't use auto-renew.

## If you're struggling to pay

If cost is the issue, don't just let it lapse — **talk to us**. You may be able to
**downgrade** to a cheaper plan (see *Upgrading or downgrading a service*), or we can
discuss options. Open a ticket before termination, when we have the most room to
help.

## Troubleshooting

- **Paid but still suspended:** allow a little time; if it persists, open a ticket
  with the invoice number.
- **Kept getting charged after cancelling:** make sure the service was actually
  cancelled, not just unpaid; see *Cancellations and refunds*.
- **Service terminated and data gone:** unfortunately termination removes data —
  which is why we send reminders; restore from any backup you kept.
