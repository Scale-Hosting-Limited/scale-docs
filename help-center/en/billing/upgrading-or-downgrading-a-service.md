Needs change — you outgrow a plan, or you realise you over-bought. Most services can
move up or down a tier without starting over. This explains how, and how the pricing
is handled when you switch mid-cycle.

## When to upgrade

Upgrade when your service is **consistently hitting its limits** — RAM, CPU, disk, or
player/traffic capacity — even after tuning (see *Reducing lag and monitoring
resources* and *Out of memory and resource limits*). No amount of configuration makes
hardware do more than it can; more resources is the honest fix.

## When to downgrade

Downgrade if you're **comfortably under** your limits and want to save money. Just
make sure the smaller plan still covers your real peak usage — downsizing too far
trades a bit of saving for lag.

## How to change plan

1. Go to **Services** and open the service (or find the **Upgrade/Downgrade**
   option).
2. Choose the new plan. The panel shows the **price difference**.
3. Confirm and pay any difference. The change usually applies quickly; some products
   need a **restart** to pick up new resources.

For bigger changes (a different product or game), see *Reinstalling or switching
games*.

## How proration works

When you change mid-cycle, we adjust for the time already paid:

- **Upgrading:** you typically pay the **difference** for the remainder of the
  current cycle, then the new rate going forward.
- **Downgrading:** the lower rate applies going forward; any credit for the unused
  difference is handled per our billing terms (sometimes as **account credit** — see
  *Account credit and promo codes*).

The exact figure is shown before you confirm, so there are no surprises.

## Will I lose data?

- **Resource upgrades/downgrades** (more/less RAM, CPU, disk) normally **keep your
  data** — it's the same service with different limits.
- **Downsizing disk** below what you're using can be a problem — reduce your usage
  first, or you may not be able to downgrade.
- **Switching product/game** is different and can wipe files — back up first (see
  *Reinstalling or switching games*).

Back up before any significant change, just in case (see your product's backup
guide).

## After changing

- **Restart** if the product needs it to apply new resources.
- **Check the resource graphs** to confirm the new limits are in effect.
- **Re-test** your service under normal load.

## Troubleshooting

- **New resources not applied:** restart the service; if still wrong, open a ticket.
- **Can't downgrade:** you're probably using more disk (or another resource) than the
  smaller plan allows — reduce usage first.
- **Unexpected charge/credit:** open the invoice to see the proration; ask us if it's
  unclear (see *Understanding your invoice*).
